CU in Space

CU Boulder's competitive rocketry club, designing and building a rocket for competition. I'm a member of the Guidance, Navigation & Control subteam.

01

Overview

CU in Space is a student-run competitive rocketry club at CU Boulder that designs, builds, and flies rockets for competition. The team spans the full vehicle — structures, propulsion, recovery, and avionics — with subteams owning each part of the design.

I'm on the Guidance, Navigation & Control subteam, which is responsible for how the vehicle senses its state and how that information is used in flight.

02

My focus — GNC

The GNC subteam owns the rocket's sensing and state estimation — building on the state-estimation and sensor-fusion work from my fixed-wing autopilot.

Navigation

State estimation

Fuse inertial, barometric, and GPS data into an estimate of the rocket's position, velocity, and attitude through the flight.

Avionics

Sensor integration

Select, integrate, and calibrate the flight sensors and get clean, trustworthy data off them.

Analysis

Flight data

Process telemetry and post-flight data to validate the estimate and inform the next design iteration.

03

Technical scope

Outline of the GNC work, to be refined with the team:

  • Sensor suite: IMU, barometer, and GPS selection, integration, and calibration.
  • State estimation: sensor fusion for position, velocity, and attitude during boost, coast, and descent.
  • Flight computer: onboard software to run the estimator and log data reliably under flight loads.
  • Telemetry & recovery: data downlink and support for the recovery sequence.
  • Validation: bench testing and post-flight data analysis to verify the system.
