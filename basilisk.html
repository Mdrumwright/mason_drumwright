Basilisk Astrodynamics Simulation

As a Research Assistant in CU Boulder's Autonomous Vehicle Systems Lab, I extend the open-source Basilisk astrodynamics framework by expanding its spacecraft dynamics to model more complex multibody configurations.

01

Overview

The purpose of the Basilisk astrodynamics simulation framework is to enable sophisticated spacecraft simulations to be rapidly created from a series of pre-built modules. Consider a deep space mission where the spacecraft must perform a series of science, communication, station keeping and orbit maneuver tasks. Such operations require not only the spacecraft orbital and attitude dynamics to be modeled, but also sub-components such as reaction wheels, thruster, flexible panels, and fuel slosh. Beyond the mechanical components the spacecraft also contains algorithms that determine the orientation, perform closed loop maneuvers, process sensor data etc. Depending on the depth of analysis being performed, the battery, data storage or communication devices might have to be modeled.

My work focuses on extending the dynamics that the simulation can model. This requires deriving the general equations of motion for complex multibody spacecraft and implementing them as new modules in the framework.

02

My focus

Extending Basilisk's spacecraft dynamics to support complex multibody configurations.

Dynamics

Branching dynamics

Extending spacecraft dynamics to support the branching dynamics of rigid-body subcomponents relative to the hub. Deriving the general translational and rotational equations of motion for complex spinning and translating systems.

Framework

Decoupling dynamics

Implementing decoupling dynamics in the open-source Basilisk framework, expanding simulation capability for complex multibody spacecraft configurations.

Application

Panel deployments

Simulating complex solar-panel deployments with Basilisk's prescribed-motion state effector module to analyze the results of different panel geometries.

03

Technical scope

Outline of the work:

  • Equations of motion: derive the general translational and rotational EOM for complex spinning and translating multibody systems.
  • Multibody dynamics: implement branching and decoupling dynamics for rigid-body subcomponents relative to the spacecraft hub.
  • Deployment analysis: simulate solar-panel deployments with the prescribed-motion state effector module across different panel geometries.
  • C++ / Python: build modules in the C/C++ core and expose them through the Python interface.
  • Version control: manage work in a shared Git repository to track edits and fix bugs quickly.
